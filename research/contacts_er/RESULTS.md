# Contact nayose: which architecture earns its place

Data: synthetic CRM contacts in the shape of the real ones (contacts, phones, addresses joined on
CONT_ID; names in kanji, kana and romaji generated from the kana; birth date always filled; gender
sometimes blank or wrong; 1-23 phones and addresses per contact; every field but CONT_ID and birth
date can be blank). Several contacts are one person (merge only). Generated by
`custom_nodes/agent_workflow_studio/nayose_bench/contacts.py` from the benchmark's population:
households, marriages (surname changes), moves through real municipality mergers, twins, look-alike
strangers, shared switchboard numbers and care-home addresses, and channel-specific dirt (variant
kanji, homophone kanji, guessed readings, kana widths, long vowels, OCR, typos, phone formats,
address notations). Two datasets: seed 7 (9,883 contacts, 5,469 people, 7,966 true pairs) and
seed 11 (9,596 contacts, 5,312 people, 7,774 true pairs).

Rule: a component is kept only if it improves the final pairwise F1 on **both** datasets; every
step shows the confusion matrix at the three stages it can change. TP/FP/FN count pairs of
contacts (FN = true pairs not merged). Dropped components are retried once on the final pipeline.
Code: `research/contacts_er/` (prep, bench, run, ladder, leave_one_out, ablate, features_min).

## The architecture

1. **Standardize**: kanji variants folded; kana full-width, small->large, ヅ/ヂ->ズ/ジ, long vowels
   folded, plus an unvoiced key (a guessed reading meets); phones canonical (+81, a lost leading 0,
   placeholders dropped); addresses parsed against a town registry (renamed municipalities -> current
   name) into locality, block number, room.
2. **Candidates** (deterministic + sparse): birth date + each name part (kana and kanji), the full
   name alone, swapped day/month; any shared phone; any shared house (block-level address); groups
   over a cap are purged (a switchboard, a care home); plus a sparse q-gram kNN on the name.
3. **Compare**: name (kana or kanji; similarity, swaps, given-name or surname agreement), birth date
   (exact, day/month swapped, digits apart, years apart), phones and houses shared (with how common
   the shared value is), best address similarity, same locality.
4. **Decide**: day 0 = rules (precision 0.985-0.988) and a review queue ordered by the model's
   uncertainty; a logistic regression retrained on every review. 300 reviews -> F1 0.989; 1,000 -> 0.995.
5. **Cluster**: guarded: strongest pairs first, never joining contacts decided different, size cap.

Against the proposed deterministic -> sparse & dense index -> reranker -> decision engine: deterministic
and sparse keys stay; **standardization and set keys** (shared phone/house) are the largest gains after
the model; **dense and the reranker** do not earn their place on this data; the decision engine is a
**supervised model on actively chosen reviews**, followed by **guarded clustering**.

## Remaining errors (final pipeline)

- False merges: look-alike strangers (same name, same birth date): 29 / 17.
- Missed: swapped name fields (recall 0.91-0.93), swapped day/month (0.93-0.98), contacts with no
  name in either script (0.97).

## Caveats

- Synthetic data from one generator: two seeds test stability, not realism. The decisive check is a
  labelled sample of the real data, matched locally (it never leaves the machine).
- Reviews are simulated by the answer key (a perfect reviewer).
- Scale: 10k contacts per dataset. Candidate keys are linear with purge caps; features ran at ~30k
  pairs/s (about 2 minutes for 1M contacts' ~2.5M pairs, extrapolated). A 1M run is not done yet.

# Part 2: does it hold up to very challenging data?

The first part used one dirt model. To see whether the architecture survives real-world mess,
the generator got stress profiles (`nayose_bench/contacts.py` PROFILES), and every change again had
to earn its place by the confusion matrix:

| Profile | What it adds |
|---|---|
| dirty | 2.5x every kind of dirt |
| sparse | 4x blank fields (kana, kanji, given name, gender, phones, addresses) |
| lookalike | 5x look-alike strangers, a population heavy in common names |
| wild | CRM mess never modelled before: honorifics (様/殿) and maiden-name notes (（旧姓 X）) in name fields, the whole name in the surname field, romaji in the kanji field, four gender code styles, placeholder birth dates, phone extensions and two numbers in one field, 同上 and postal-code-only addresses |
| hard | all of it at once (55% of contacts without a phone, 38% without kana) |

## How much of the data can be matched at all

Before blaming the pipeline, the share of true pairs that share any identifying evidence beyond
birth date and gender (a similar name in either script, a phone, a house):

| Data | Name or phone/house shared | Birth date only | Nothing |
|---|---|---|---|
| base | 99.1% | 0.8% | 0.0% |
| dirty | 94.1% | 5.2% | 0.7% |
| sparse | 93.2% | 6.4% | 0.4% |
| hard | **80.7%** | 16.9% | 2.4% |

On the hard data about 19% of true pairs cannot be linked safely at scale (a birth date is shared
by ~34 people per date at 1M contacts). Recall there is bounded near 0.81.

## The proposed architecture, as specified, against the measured one

| Variant | Base 10k | Hard 7 10k | Hard 11 10k |
|---|---|---|---|
| proposed: raw, deterministic + sparse + dense, reranker, model, no clustering | 0.9902 | 0.8761 | 0.8719 |
| + standardization | 0.9924 | 0.9111 | 0.9073 |
| + phone/house set keys | 0.9936 | 0.9161 | 0.9049 |
| + guarded clustering | 0.9920 | 0.9219 | 0.9103 |
| - reranker | identical | identical | identical |
| - dense | **0.9951** | 0.8880 | 0.8803 |

At 10k contacts the dense index looked like a clear win on hard data (+0.03). At 100k it is not:

| 100k contacts, 3,000 reviews | F1 | P / R | TP / FP / FN | Pairs | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hard, without dense | **0.8417** | 0.904 / 0.787 | 66741 / 7079 / 18031 | 1.06M | 61 s |
| hard, with dense | 0.8402 | 0.907 / 0.782 | 66309 / 6767 / 18463 | 1.71M | 246 s |
| base, without dense | **0.9897** | 0.987 / 0.992 | 78391 / 1008 / 624 | 0.82M | 61 s |
| base, with dense | 0.9890 | 0.986 / 0.992 | 78367 / 1097 / 648 | 1.39M | 250 s |

Dense finds pairs that share little more than a birth date; at 10k a birth date is nearly unique,
at 100k it is not, and the model rightly stops trusting it. Its gain was a small-population artifact.

## Scale (100k contacts)

- Birth-date frequency as a feature: no effect at 10k; at 100k hard, with 3,000 reviews, it cuts
  false merges from 11,953 to 6,750 (F1 0.8158 -> 0.8431). Kept.
- Reviews: 3,000 -> 10,000 reviews: hard F1 0.8435 -> 0.8572 (FP 7,207 -> 4,672); base 0.9903 ->
  0.9954 (FP 1,116 -> 348), at the same recall.
- Decision threshold (precision vs recall, a business choice), hard 100k, 10,000 reviews:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.935 | 0.791 | 4,672 | 17,683 |
| 0.7 | 0.936 | 0.788 | 4,561 | 17,955 |
| 0.85 | 0.947 | 0.765 | 3,658 | 19,930 |
| 0.95 | 0.963 | 0.726 | 2,344 | 23,244 |

## Corroboration rule (a policy option, not the default)

Name + birth date alone never auto-merge; a merge needs a second identifier (phone, house, unit,
locality, a stated maiden name); the rest goes to review. 100k contacts, 10,000 reviews:

| | F1 | P / R | TP / FP / FN | Sent to review |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hard: model | 0.8610 | 0.933 / 0.799 | 67750 / 4850 / 17022 | - |
| hard: corroboration rule | 0.6449 | 0.985 / 0.479 | 40640 / 623 / 44132 | 34,636 (87% true) |
| base: model | 0.9950 | 0.995 / 0.995 | 78596 / 379 / 419 | - |
| base: corroboration rule | 0.9799 | 0.9993 / 0.961 | 75941 / 53 / 3074 | 7,189 (91% true) |

It cuts false merges by 87% but moves the work to people. Use it where a wrong merge is very costly.

## The architecture that holds up

1. **Standardize**: kanji variants, kana (small/large, ヅ/ヂ, long vowels, an unvoiced key), kana OCR
   repair, phones canonical, addresses parsed against a town registry (renamed municipalities);
   readings learned from the data (kanji -> kana) and whole names split, so kanji-only and kana-only
   contacts meet.
2. **Candidates (deterministic + sparse)**: birth date + each name part (both scripts, maiden
   alias), full name alone, swapped day/month; any shared phone; any shared house; birth date +
   locality; sparse q-gram on names; oversized groups purged.
3. **Compare**: names, birth date (with how many share it), phones and houses (with how common they
   are), addresses, locality.
4. **Decide**: a logistic regression trained on actively chosen reviews (day 0: rules), disputed
   reviews audited by a second reviewer; the threshold set by the business's precision/recall choice;
   optional corroboration rule for precision-first uses.
5. **Cluster**: guarded (never joins contacts decided different; size cap).

Not kept: dense embedding index (a 10k artifact; no gain at 100k, 4x the time), cross-encoder
reranker (no effect anywhere), Fellegi-Sunter in every form tried, gradient boosting, gender and
name-frequency features.

| Result | Base | Hard (very challenging) |
|---|---|---|
| 10k contacts, 1,000 reviews | F1 0.996 | F1 0.89 / 0.88 (recall at the evidence ceiling) |
| 100k contacts, 10,000 reviews | F1 0.995 (FP 379, FN 419) | F1 0.861 (P 0.933, R 0.799) |

## What is still unproven

- 1M contacts (not run yet; ask first).
- The generator is ours: real data will have mess we did not imagine. The decisive check is a
  labelled sample of the real data, matched on this machine.
- The remaining false merges are look-alike strangers (same name and birth date): about 2,300 of
  4,850 at 100k hard. Only more evidence (or the corroboration rule) separates them.

## Companies and sole proprietors in the generator

- Companies sit in the name fields, CONT_TYPE says 2 (wrong for ~4%): legal form full / (株) / ㈱ /
  omitted / moved, branches (東京支店, 本社, 営業所), departments in FIRST_NAME, kana with or without
  カブシキガイシャ or as カ); BIRTH_DATE the founding date for ~85% (else blank or another date),
  gender filled by mistake ~4%; head-office and branch phones and addresses.
- Sole proprietors (8% of adults 25-80): about half their contacts after opening are the shop
  (藤江不動産), flagged either way, with the owner's birth date or the shop's founding date and the
  shop's phone; the answer key says shop and owner are one entity.

owner (45% on hard data); the rest carry the shop's founding date and the shop's own phone and no
evidence pointing to the owner.

| Renamed (処理区分 11) | 1,378 old -> new names | 6%: contacts before the rename date carry the old name |
| Head office moved (処理区分 12) | 8,402 old -> new addresses | 6%: contacts before the move carry the old head office |
| Absorbed by a merger (closing reason 11, successor number) | 19,635 (sampled) | 6%: the absorbed company is its own closed entity; 30% of successors take over its phone number |
| Same name, different company | 54,572 companies in same-name groups | 6% of groups, up to 3 strangers each |
| Group companies at one address with one name stem | 14,325 | 6% of groups, up to 4 siblings sharing the building and switchboard |

Policy defaults (the answer key; change them with the profile):
- an absorbed company stays a separate (closed) entity linked to its successor; `merger_policy:
  "successor"` makes its contacts belong to the successor instead;
- group companies are always separate entities.

English names are rare in the registry: 686 of 300,000 companies have one. About 6% of their
contacts use it.

## What remains (company hard, one seed, `company_errors.py`)

- **Missed company pairs: all are candidates the model scored apart; none was missed by blocking.**
  So a better index (embeddings included) cannot recover them. They are contacts that stack
  several kinds of dirt: an old head office after a move, missing kana, the registration date in
  place of the founding date, bank-transfer kana, a department in FIRST_NAME. Company hard 7 has
  68, company hard 11 has 34.
- **False company merges** (31 and 14):
  - an absorbed company with its successor (it took over the phone number);
  - sibling group companies in one building on one switchboard;
  - a few same-name strangers.

  Only a registry lookup (corporate number, closing date, successor) separates these. The CRM
  does not carry that. Each one is a pair for the review queue.

## The architecture after Part 4

Unchanged in shape: standardize -> deterministic + set + sparse + locality candidates -> compare ->
logistic regression on actively chosen reviews (audited) -> guarded clustering. Companies add
two things:
- standardization: addressee, 御中 and department removal, and a folded name core;
- a core-equality feature.

Embeddings are not used:
- 10k people: an artifact;
- 100k people: no gain at 4x the time;
- companies: the remaining misses are already candidates, and the dense channel hurt company hard 11.
